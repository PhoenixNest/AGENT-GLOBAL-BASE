## Compliance Verdict

**Below Standard — P0 gaps present.**

Two defects meet ASGF's P0 wording on their own terms, both reproduced live this session rather
than inferred: the dangerous-action gate refuses every task on win32 because a platform
conditional leaves an empty regex in its denylist (B2), and the error boundary cannot recognise a
real provider 429 at all, because it defines its own `RateLimitError` and never maps the SDK's —
so the most common production failure class in LLM applications falls through to an untyped
`UNKNOWN_ERROR` and the backoff layer never engages (B1). Both are small, contained fixes; neither
is a design flaw, which is precisely why they are worth fixing before anything else on this list.

The single biggest lever is **typed-error fidelity at the boundary**. The retry, jitter, breaker,
and token-bucket machinery around it is at parity with current external practice and is not the
problem — it is downstream of a classification step that currently discards the type information
all of it depends on. Fix R2 and R1, and the remaining findings are ordinary hardening work.

### Evidence Completeness Statement

Of 12 benchmark rows, 11 carry at least one `Verified` external source and 1 (B7, client-side
proactive rate limiting) is `Unassessed — no source`. Three searches returned nothing usable and
are logged rather than dropped: a claimed rate-limit-share statistic that no retrieved page
supports (S2); a candidate multi-agent retry-storm analysis whose host does not resolve, so it
could not be read and cannot support anything (S10); and client-side token-bucket throttling as a
named enterprise pattern (S19). B3 additionally carries a `Contradicted` source (S1) and reports
that source's actual position — that circuit breakers are common but not universal in production
LLM gateways — rather than the position originally sought.

The dimension I most wanted to assess and could not is **whether a proactive client-side limiter
is considered good or bad practice externally**; the retrieved guidance simply does not address it,
and B7 says so instead of guessing. I also want to flag a coverage limit in what _is_ assessed:
B10 and B11 compare a stubbed reference implementation against production controls, so their
verdicts describe what an adopter would have to build, not a live exposure.

The Reviewer named in Metadata has completed the excerpt-to-claim check, including reproducing
both P0 findings live on this machine (see Metadata). This assessment is signed off.
